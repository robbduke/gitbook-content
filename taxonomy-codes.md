# Taxonomy Codes

Taxonomy codes are a foundational part of the healthcare credentialing process. Understanding what they are, why they matter, and how they are assigned will help ensure your organization’s applications, claims, and enrollments are accurate and compliant.

### What Is a Taxonomy Code? <a href="#undefined" id="undefined"></a>

A **taxonomy code** is a unique, 10-character alphanumeric code that classifies healthcare providers by type, classification, and area of specialization. These codes are used to identify what kind of provider you are—such as a physician, nurse practitioner, behavioral health clinic, or specialty practice—when you apply for a National Provider Identifier (NPI) and when you submit claims to insurance payers.

Taxonomy codes are structured in three levels:

* **Provider Type:** The broadest category (e.g., physician, clinic, supplier)
* **Classification:** A more specific category within the type (e.g., internal medicine, family practice)
* **Specialization:** The most detailed level, indicating a particular focus or expertise (e.g., geriatric medicine, addiction psychiatry).

### Why Are Taxonomy Codes Important? <a href="#undefined" id="undefined"></a>

* **Credentialing and Enrollment:** You must select at least one taxonomy code when applying for an NPI. Insurance payers use this code to verify your specialty and qualifications during credentialing and contracting.
* **Claims Processing:** Taxonomy codes are required on many insurance claims. They help payers process claims correctly, apply the right fee schedules, and ensure only eligible providers are reimbursed for certain services.
* **Regulatory Compliance:** Taxonomy codes are mandated by HIPAA and are essential for compliance with federal and state regulations. Using the correct code helps prevent claim denials, delays, and audit issues.
* **Network Adequacy and Reporting:** Payers and regulators use taxonomy codes to assess network adequacy, track provider types, and analyze healthcare delivery trends.

### Who Assigns Taxonomy Codes? <a href="#undefined" id="undefined"></a>

* **Self-Selection:** Providers and organizations select their own taxonomy codes when applying for an NPI through the National Plan & Provider Enumeration System (NPPES).
* **National Uniform Claim Committee (NUCC):** The NUCC maintains and updates the official taxonomy code set. The code set is updated twice a year (January and July) to reflect new specialties and changes in healthcare practice.
* **Payers and Credentialing Bodies:** May require you to confirm or update your taxonomy code(s) during credentialing, recredentialing, or claims submission.

### How to Find and Use Your Taxonomy Code <a href="#undefined" id="undefined"></a>

* **NPI Application:** When applying for an NPI, select the taxonomy code(s) that best match your provider type and specialty. You may choose more than one, but must designate a primary code.
* **NPI Registry:** Your selected taxonomy codes are listed in the NPI Registry and can be updated as your practice evolves.
* **Claims Submission:** Always use the taxonomy code that matches the services provided on each claim to avoid denials or payment delays.

### Best Practices <a href="#undefined" id="undefined"></a>

* **Be Accurate:** Choose the taxonomy code that most closely matches your actual specialty and services.
* **Keep Codes Updated:** Update your taxonomy codes in NPPES and with payers if your specialty or services change.
* **Consistency Matters:** Ensure your taxonomy code is consistent across all applications, claims, and credentialing documents.
* **Check Payer Requirements:** Some payers may have specific requirements for taxonomy codes—review their guidelines before submitting claims or applications.
